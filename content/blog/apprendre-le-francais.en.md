A two-minute video can fill a whole French practice session. Before pressing play, choose a question: **who is speaking, what are they talking about, and where are they?** These three free resources help you practise different skills.

## Start with a video

[TV5MONDE – Apprendre le français](https://apprendre.tv5monde.com/fr) organises exercises by level. Beginners can look for **Première classe** at A1. On the first viewing, identify the setting and the people. On the second, listen for an expression you could use to introduce yourself or ask for information.

## Listen to a news story

[RFI’s Journal en français facile](https://francaisfacile.rfi.fr/fr/podcasts/journal-en-fran%C3%A7ais-facile/) publishes transcripts alongside the audio. A whole broadcast can still feel demanding. Pick one story, listen without reading, then check what you understood against the transcript. A place name or a repeated word can give you a starting point for a summary.

## Try an official DELF sample

[France Éducation international](https://www.france-education-international.fr/diplome/delf-junior-scolaire) explains the DELF junior/scolaire and provides sample tasks by level. Choose one listening or reading task. Before checking the answer key, write down the clue that supports your answer. The sample materials are free. Registering for the exam is separate.

## A fifteen-minute session

1. **Three minutes** to listen to or read an extract and note the topic.
2. **Five minutes** to revisit it and check two expressions in context.
3. **Five minutes** to write two sentences about the story and one question for a classmate.
4. **Two minutes** to read your summary aloud.

Keep that first summary. A week later, return to the same extract and try telling the story without your notes.
